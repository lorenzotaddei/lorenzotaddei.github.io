Woodworking Projects

Furniture

Chest of Drawers ×

Size: 1.5m x 1m, with drawers.

A long project, with the design created from scratch. Every joint is fixed with dowels — there are no visible screws anywhere on the piece except at the legs, where they're hidden from view. Still to be varnished.

Materials: Fir wood

Tools used: jig, table saw, drill, level, hand plane (to keep the joints flat and free of bumps).

Process

1. Cutting the pieces — given how complex the structure is, most pieces had to be cut to the millimeter.

2. Assembling the frame — pieces joined together, drawers not yet in. No screws in the structure — every joint is fixed with dowels, with the dowel holes drilled using a jig.

3. Fitting the drawers — drawers fitted into the frame using internal guides. The only screws on the whole piece are at the legs, and none of them are visible.

Current state

Structure complete, still to be varnished.

Nightstand Drawer Unit ×

Materials: fir, beech runners for the drawers [a harder, smoother wood than the frame — swap this if it's not what you used], brass handles, glue, nails.

Tools used: band saw, router with a 45° edge bit, glue, nails.

Process

1. The frame — the main structure, glued together in fir.

2. The drawers — three drawers that slide independently. Inside, I fitted beech runners — a harder wood that lets them slide more smoothly than fir alone would.

3. Edging the drawer fronts — the front edges of the drawers were cut at 45° with a router.

4. Top and bottom — fitted with the same 45° edge detail as the drawer fronts.

5. Finishing touches — an open drawer seen from above, with the brass handles added.

Small Hinged Box ×

Materials: cherry wood, wood glue, finishing oil to bring out the grain.

Tools used: table saw, chisel, router with a round-over bit (used on the corners, to make them soft and rounded to the touch), glue.

Process: Built entirely in cherry, with no screws — every joint is glued. The corners were rounded off with a router to feel soft and smooth rather than sharp. The lid opens and closes on a headless pin fitted into both sides, acting as a simple pivot hinge.

Cruet Holder ×

Materials: solid wood block [fill in wood type], finishing oil/wood stain, wood glue.

Tools used: band saw, chisel, drill, sandpaper.

Process

1. Marking out — started from a rectangular wood block, with the shape marked out in pencil.

2. Cutting the curves — cut on a band saw. Since a band saw doesn't cut tight curves cleanly freehand, I made a series of closely spaced parallel "comb" cuts, then leveled the surface down with a chisel.

3. The hole — the central hole for the cruet bottle was drilled out, and the base was glued on without screws.

Final Result

Sanded and finished with wood oil to bring out the grain.
